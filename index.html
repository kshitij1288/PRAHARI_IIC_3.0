PROJECT RATIONALE

Bridging Regional Forecasts with Slope-Level Reality

How PRAHARI proposes to complement district-level meteorological advisories with hyper-local, community-accessible slope signals in vulnerable sectors like Wayanad.

THE LOCAL GAP

Why District-Level Alerts Need Slope Context

In mountainous terrain (such as Wayanad, Uttarakhand, or the Western Ghats), the India Meteorological Department (IMD) and State Disaster Management Authorities (SDMAs) provide invaluable regional weather bulletins (e.g. orange or red monsoon alerts spanning hundreds of square kilometers).

However, whether a specific slope slips depends on hyper-localized geotechnical conditions: localized rain shadow cloudbursts, pre-existing groundwater saturation, slope angle of repose, drainage blockages, and micro-movements in soil strata. A village 5 km away may remain completely stable while an adjacent slope reaches critical saturation.

"Macro-level satellite radar tells us a storm is above the district; local slope sensors tell a village whether their specific hillside is absorbing or shedding that water."
THE PROPOSED CONCEPT

What PRAHARI Proposes

PRAHARI (meaning Sentinel or Watchman) is an open IoT slope sentinel framework developed by <HYPNEXIS>:

  • Continuous Edge Monitoring: Measures subsurface soil moisture across dual depths (S1 & S2), rainfall presence, tilt displacement, and micro-vibrations.
  • Contextual Assessment: Evaluates local readings alongside official IMD/SDMA regional alerts to estimate a composite risk score (0–100).
  • Live Backend Integration: Connects to https://api.prahari.space to stream live ESP32 prediction logs.
  • Community Empowerment: Equips Village Disaster Management Committees (VDMC) and panchayat focal points with local observational indicators.

Designed to Work Alongside IMD & SDMA, Not Instead of Them

Official government agencies possess national radar, satellite telemetry, and weather models. PRAHARI is conceptualized to supply the "last 100 meters" of ground-truth observation—giving village committees early observational signals to clean culverts, monitor road cuts, and prepare before disaster strikes.

DATA ARCHITECTURE

Sense → Assess → Communicate

A three-stage data pipeline designed for bandwidth efficiency, edge resilience, and village-level accessibility.

01
EDGE SAMPLING

SENSE

The ESP32 node awakes from deep sleep every 60 seconds (or immediately upon tilt/rain interrupt). It captures synchronized readings across all sensor channels:

  • Samples accelerometer (ADXL345) for static pitch/roll and vibration energy.
  • Reads dual capacitive probes for volumetric soil moisture (S1 & S2).
  • Polls tipping bucket count, BMP180 barometric delta, and DHT22 climate metrics.
  • Applies edge-level median filtering to discard mechanical sensor noise.
Output: Compact telemetry packet posted to /data
→
02
EVALUATION ENGINE

ASSESS

The backend at api.prahari.space runs the proposed Random Forest scoring algorithm to combine multi-dimensional slope parameters:

  • Cross-references cumulative rainfall against soil moisture saturation curves.
  • Checks rate-of-change on tilt angles (Δtilt/Δtime) to identify plastic creep.
  • Ingests regional IMD / SDMA advisory status to weigh background meteorological risk.
  • Outputs a normalized 0–100 composite risk score with confidence intervals.
Output: Proposed 0–100 Risk Score & Tier
→
03
COMMUNITY READINESS

COMMUNICATE

Information is delivered in simple, actionable formats to local stakeholders without requiring complex technical interpretation:

  • Presents data on a low-bandwidth local village dashboard (like this one and prahari.space).
  • Flags initial review states (60% Advisory / 85% Critical Review) for village volunteers.
  • Prompts local disaster committees to inspect drainage channels and road culverts.
  • Logs verifiable telemetry for post-monsoon geotechnical calibration.

ESP32 Binary Struct (C/C++)

sizeof = 32 Bytes

Packed fixed-width struct transmitted over LoRa/SPI. Maximizes radio battery lifetime by avoiding verbose JSON at the physical node. 

typedef struct __attribute__((packed)) {

MACHINE LEARNING CONCEPT

Proposed Random Forest Risk Synthesis

Why an ensemble of decision trees is proposed to model non-linear geotechnical slope interactions from multi-modal sensor inputs.

Why Random Forest for Landslide Risk?

Slope failure is rarely caused by a single isolated variable. For example:

  • High Rainfall + Dry Sandy Soil: Water drains quickly; immediate risk remains low.
  • Moderate Rainfall + 90% Saturated Soil: Pore-water pressure spikes; soil shear strength collapses.
  • Micro-Tilt Change Alone: Could be wind buffeting or animal contact; requires corroborating moisture and vibration signals to confirm ground movement.

A Random Forest ensemble model handles these non-linear cross-correlations exceptionally well. By aggregating predictions across multiple decision trees trained on diverse subsets of geotechnical variables, it prevents false triggers caused by any single faulty sensor while remaining lightweight enough for edge or gateway micro-inference.

Proposed Initial Review Thresholds

PRAHARI introduces a dual-tier review threshold logic for community preparedness. Crucially, these are unvalidated initial baselines that require site-specific local field calibration.

Proposed Preparedness Steps:
  • Local disaster committee members check drainage paths & debris basins.
  • Verify latest regional IMD radar & SDMA meteorological bulletins.
  • Node telemetry transmission frequency increases from 15 min to 2 min.
  • Community focal points confirm communication channels with village elders.

TIER 2 REVIEW
85%

Critical Technical Review Threshold

Proposed trigger when extreme soil saturation occurs concurrently with detected micro-tilt displacement or anomalous vibration spikes.

Proposed Preparedness Steps:
  • Flag urgent technical inspection condition for local disaster committee.
  • Direct visual verification of hillside cracks and tension scarps by trained volunteers.
  • Consult official district disaster administration for official instructions.
  • Prepare standby shelter kits in accordance with standard village safety protocols.
UNVALIDATED PROPOSAL

MANDATORY NOTICE: LOCAL FIELD CALIBRATION IS REQUIRED

The 60% and 85% thresholds shown above are unvalidated mathematical benchmarks created for hackathon demonstration. No two hillsides behave identically:

Slope angle of repose (e.g., 28° vs 55°), soil porosity, hydraulic permeability, bedrock depth, shear strength parameters (cohesion c and internal friction angle φ), and historical rainfall-trigger thresholds vary dramatically across Wayanad, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, and Sikkim.

These thresholds must NEVER be deployed in the field without empirical geotechnical soil sampling, multi-season baseline data logging, and validation alongside certified disaster management geologists.

CRITICAL INFORMATION

Safety, Boundaries & Limitations

PRAHARI is strictly an academic hackathon proposal. Responsible deployment mandates complete transparency regarding what this system is—and what it is not.

Not a Public Warning Service

PRAHARI is not authorized, certified, or intended to operate as a public early-warning siren or disaster broadcasting platform. It must never be relied upon as the primary safety mechanism for human settlements.

Does Not Issue Evacuation Orders

Evacuation orders are exclusively the legal, administrative, and operational prerogative of designated civil authorities (District Collectors, SDMAs, NDMAs). PRAHARI will never issue evacuation commands or trigger autonomous alarms.

Does Not Connect to Real Hardware Yet

While the live mode connects to https://api.prahari.space, all data should be treated as experimental research packets. No certified safety-critical life-support operations are automated.

Must Not Replace Official Guidance

In all meteorological events and landslide alerts, citizens and administrators must adhere strictly to advisories from the India Meteorological Department (IMD), National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), and local administration.
